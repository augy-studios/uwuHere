uwuHere

Privacy

Last updated 16 November 2026

uwuHere is made by Augy Studios. This page says what uwuHere keeps about hosts and attendees, where it goes, and when it's deleted. It covers the website at here.uwuapps.org, the Android app (from Google Play or GitHub), and the Windows, macOS and Linux apps.

The short version

  • No ads, and nothing about you is sold. The apps have no analytics or tracking, and don't use your device's advertising ID. The website counts visits with Google Analytics.
  • There are no accounts. An event keeps the names people type to check in, how they checked in and when, and nothing else about them.
  • Your location is used once, to work out how far you are from the host, and only that distance is kept. Never where you were.
  • Every event is deleted 7 days after it ends, or 30 days after it was made if nobody ends it.

1. If you host an event

Making an event keeps, on uwuHere's server:

  • what you set: its name, the button's words and colour, the extra field's name, the ways people can check in, and the distance for location check-in;
  • if location check-in is on, the event's spot: where your device was when you made the event, or moved the spot, with how accurate that was;
  • if you set a password, a one-way scrambled form of it (bcrypt with a secret only the server knows), never the password itself. Passwords are checked against known data breaches by sending only the first 5 characters of their SHA-1 to Have I Been Pwned;
  • whether it's paused, when it was made and when it ended.

Your device is given a host key for the event, kept in its own storage, which lets it manage the event. There's nothing else about you: no name, email or account.

2. If you check in

Checking in keeps, with the event:

  • the name you type, and the extra field if the host asked for one (such as a student ID);
  • how you checked in (same wifi, location, tapping phones, Bluetooth, the host's hotspot, or added by the host) and when, and when you left, if you said you left early;
  • for a location check-in, how far you were from the event's spot, in metres. Not where you were;
  • a scrambled form (SHA-256) of a random ID your device makes for itself, so the same device checks in once. The host never sees the ID.

The host sees the list, and can download it as a spreadsheet (CSV). What they do with their copy is up to them.

3. How each way of checking in works

  • Same wifi: your device and the host's open screen are introduced by PeerJS's public service, which sees both IP addresses and the event's code, then talk directly, encrypted, over WebRTC. Each asks a STUN server (Google's or Cloudflare's) for its own public address. There is no relay: your name goes straight to the host's device, which saves it.
  • Location: your device's location is read once, when you press the button, and sent to the server, which works out the distance, keeps that, and drops the location.
  • Tapping phones: the host's Android phone hands your phone a link with a one-time token over NFC. Only the token's code is kept, so it can't be used twice.
  • Bluetooth (the Android app on both phones, with Google Play services): the phones find each other with Google's Nearby Connections, with no internet needed, and your name goes straight to the host's phone.
  • The host's hotspot: the host's phone becomes a wifi network with a check-in page of its own. Your name goes to the host's phone, then to the server once it's back online.

4. What stays on your device

Your device keeps, in the browser's storage on the website and the app's own storage in the apps: the events you host and their host keys, the last copy of each list (so it opens offline), check-ins waiting to be sent, your own check-ins, the name you last typed, and your theme. The first time the 2.0 Android app opens after an update from 1.0, it copies what the older app kept on the same phone into its own storage. Nothing leaves the device for that.

5. Report a bug

If you use Report a bug, what you send goes to uwuHere's server and on to Augy Studios as a message from a Telegram bot. It contains what you typed, a way to reach you if you give one, up to three pictures if you add them (a screenshot shows whatever is on that page, which can include an event's list), and what helps find the bug, shown to you before it's sent: the app's version, which app, your device and system as your browser or app describes them, and the page you were on. Never an event's code, name or list, unless a picture you add shows them.

uwuHere's server doesn't store reports. They're kept in Augy Studios' Telegram chats, which Telegram stores under its own privacy policy. To limit misuse, the server counts reports from each IP address for a minute and, where set up, for an hour. Each report gets an ID (like 20261116-7K3Q): quote it to have that report deleted.

6. Updates and notifications

  • Notifications on Android (each off until you turn it on with the bell) use Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google's push service. Your phone joins topics: one for new versions, one for news, and one for each event you host, named from its host key so nobody else can join it. uwuHere's server keeps no list of phones. "Alex checked in" notifications carry the attendee's name and the event's name.
  • The Android app from GitHub asks uwuHere's server whether there's a new version, and downloads it from GitHub, which sees your IP address. From Google Play, Google Play updates it.
  • The Windows, macOS and Linux apps check GitHub for new versions about once an hour, and, while they run, check the events you host each minute for check-in notifications.
  • The "Your event" widget on Android refreshes its event's numbers from uwuHere's server with the event's host key.

7. The website

  • Google Analytics counts visits to the website's pages: which pages are opened, roughly where from, and on what kind of device and browser. It uses cookies to tell visits apart. It's on the website only, never in the apps. Your browser's settings or an ad blocker can stop it, and uwuHere works the same without it.
  • Google Fonts serves the website's font, so Google sees your IP address when it loads. The apps carry the font themselves.
  • For same-wifi check-in, the website loads the PeerJS library from cdnjs (Cloudflare).

8. Android permissions

  • Internet: to reach uwuHere's server, and for same-wifi check-in.
  • Location: only when you press something that needs it (checking in by location, or setting an event's spot). It isn't kept.
  • NFC: for tapping phones, while a host page you have open uses it.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth and Wi-Fi; location on Android 12 and older, which Android needs for them there): only asked for when you use Bluetooth check-in or start the hotspot. It isn't used to find where you are.
  • Notifications: only asked for when you turn one on with the bell.
  • Install apps: the GitHub version only, to install its own updates when you choose Update. The Google Play version doesn't have it.
  • Keep the screen on: while a host page is taking check-ins.

uwuHere doesn't use your contacts, camera, microphone, files or advertising ID.

These companies run parts of uwuHere for us. They handle your data only to provide that part, under their own privacy policies. We don't sell or share your data with anyone else, and nothing is used for advertising.

10. Keeping and deleting your data

  • An event, its list and everyone's names on it are deleted 7 days after the host ends it, or 30 days after it was made if nobody ends it. Nothing is kept after that.
  • A host can remove anyone from the list at any time, which deletes that check-in.
  • An attendee who wants their check-in removed before then can ask the host, or email us with the event's code.
  • Uninstalling the app, or clearing the website's data, deletes what your device kept. Forgetting an event on the home page deletes this device's copy of it.
  • Bug reports can be deleted on request: email us and quote the report's ID.

11. Your choices and rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the UK or EU GDPR, or Singapore's PDPA), you may have the right to see the data we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, take a copy, or object to how it's used. Email us to ask, and we'll answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

12. Keeping it safe

Everything between your device and uwuHere's server is sent over HTTPS. The database is reachable only through uwuHere's server, and the keys to it, the host keys' secret, the password secret and the bug report bot stay on the server. No service is perfectly secure, but if something goes wrong that affects your data, we'll tell you.

13. Children

uwuHere can be used to take attendance where children are present, such as a class, by an adult host who's responsible for the list. We don't knowingly collect more than the names typed to check in, and every list is deleted as section 10 says. If you think a child's data should be removed sooner, email us with the event's code.

14. Changes to this page

If what uwuHere does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top says when. Bigger changes are also said in the apps' release notes.
